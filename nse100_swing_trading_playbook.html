What is Swing Trading?

Swing trading captures "swings" — price moves within a larger trend. Unlike day trading (exits same day) or investing (months/years), swing trading holds for 2–7 days, catching the meat of a price move. In NSE 100 stocks, a 10% move per week is achievable but requires strict discipline. You are exploiting short-term momentum, breakouts, or mean-reversion patterns.

The 10% Reality Check

10% per week compounded = 142x annually. This is not realistic every single week. Target 3–4 qualifying trades monthly, not every week. Professionals aim for 40–60% annual returns. A 10% target means you find one exceptional setup per week, not that every trade hits 10%.

Market Principles

Core principles every swing trader must internalize before executing a single trade

Principle 1 — Market Hierarchy (Always Trade With, Never Against)

Nifty 50 trend → Nifty 100 sector → Individual stock direction. A stock in a bearish market that looks bullish will usually fail. Confirm the macro first.
Bull Market Rules
Buy breakouts aggressively. Shallow dips are buying opportunities. Add to winners. Nifty above 200 DMA with rising slope.
Sideways/Choppy
Reduce position size. Trade only the clearest setups. Nifty between key MAs, no clear direction. Mean reversion better than breakouts.
Bear Market Rules
Drastically reduce exposure. Fewer trades. Cash is a position. Only short-side or wait. Nifty below 200 DMA, falling slope.

Principle 2 — Price & Volume: The Only Truth

Price is the ultimate indicator. Volume confirms intention. Every other indicator is derived from these two. Richard Wyckoff built an entire school of thought (Wyckoff Method) solely on price-volume relationships.

Bullish Volume Signals
Breakout on 2x+ average volume = institutional buying (Accumulation)
Price rising, volume rising = healthy uptrend
Price dips on low volume = weak selling, hold
Bearish Volume Signals
Price rising on declining volume = exhaustion, exit soon
Breakdown on heavy volume = Distribution, get out
High volume at resistance with little progress = sellers absorbing buyers

Principle 3 — Support, Resistance & Structure

Price remembers. Support and resistance are areas where buyers/sellers have previously congregated. The more times a level is tested, the more significant the eventual break.

▸ Previous highs become resistance — sellers who are trapped at these levels sell when price returns
▸ Broken resistance becomes support — old sellers become new buyers (flip of polarity)
▸ Round numbers (1000, 2500, 500) — psychological levels with huge option OI
▸ 52-week highs — NSE stocks that break 52W high with volume go up avg 15–25% more

Principle 4 — Trend Phases (Dow Theory + Weinstein Stages)

STAGE 1 — BASING
Price sideways, 30W MA flat. Institutions quietly accumulating. Do NOT buy yet. Watch for breakout.
STAGE 2 — ADVANCING ✓
Price above rising 30W MA. Higher highs, higher lows. Buy here. Add on pullbacks to MA.
STAGE 3 — TOPPING
Price churning at highs, 30W MA flattening. Distribution by institutions. Exit positions.
STAGE 4 — DECLINING
Price below falling 30W MA. Lower highs, lower lows. Never buy. Short only if experienced.

Principle 5 — Indian Market Specifics

◆ FII/DII Data — Check SEBI FII/DII daily data. FII buying = bullish fuel. FII selling = exit signal. Available on NSE website daily.
◆ India VIX — Volatility index. Above 20 = dangerous for swings, reduce position. Below 15 = calm market, normal sizing. Between 15–20 = standard conditions.
◆ NSE Option Chain — PCR (Put-Call Ratio) above 1.2 = bullish. Below 0.8 = bearish. Max Pain level = price will gravitate to at expiry.
◆ Expiry Effect — Weekly expiry Thursday causes volatility. Avoid new entries on Weds/Thurs unless clear setup.
◆ Budget / Result Season — Q1 results (Jul-Aug), Q2 (Oct-Nov), Q3 (Jan-Feb), Q4 (Apr-May). Stocks move 10–30% on results. Don't hold over unknown results unless you've modeled the trade.
◆ RBI Policy — Bi-monthly MPC meetings. Rate changes affect banking, NBFC, real estate stocks significantly. Mark these dates.
◆ US Market Correlation — Nifty has ~0.6 correlation with S&P 500. Global sell-offs drag NSE down regardless of domestic fundamentals. Check SGX Nifty / Gift Nifty pre-market for indication.

Stock Scanner Framework

Step-by-step filtering system to find 10% swing candidates from NSE 100

Stage 1 — Universe Filter (Daily, Takes 10 mins)

1
Market Condition Check
Nifty 50: above 200 DMA? India VIX below 20? FII data from NSE: net buyers or sellers this week? If all bad — no trading today, wait.
2
Sector Strength Scan
On TradingView/Chartink: which NSE sectors are showing highest RS (Relative Strength) vs Nifty in last 20 days? Only look for trades in top 2-3 sectors.
3
Volume Surge Filter
Screener.in filter: Volume today > 150% of 20-day average AND Price change > 2%. This catches stocks with institutional activity.
4
52-Week High Proximity
Filter stocks within 5-10% of 52-week high. Minervini's research: stocks near new highs continue higher more than those "cheap" stocks far from highs.

Stage 2 — Technical Qualification (Chart-by-Chart)

5
Trend Confirmation
EMA 20 > EMA 50 > EMA 200 on daily chart (bullish alignment). Price above all three EMAs. This is non-negotiable for long trades.
6
Pattern Identification
Is there a recognized setup? Cup with handle, Bull flag, Darvas box breakout, ascending triangle, VCP (Volatility Contraction Pattern). No clear pattern = no trade.
7
Indicator Confluence
At minimum 3 indicators must agree: RSI 50-70 range (not overbought), MACD bullish crossover or histogram expanding, volume at pattern breakout > 1.5x average.
8
Support/Stop Level
Identify the stop-loss level BEFORE entry. Must be a clear technical level — recent swing low, pattern low, or key EMA. Stop should be max 5-7% below entry for 10% target (R:R ≥ 1.5:1)

Stage 3 — Entry Execution Criteria

Entry Triggers (All 3 Preferred)
✓ Breakout candle closes above resistance on daily chart
✓ Volume on breakout day ≥ 1.5x 20-day average volume
✓ RSI crossing above 55 or confirming at 60–65 zone
✓ MACD line crossing above signal line
Enter at open next day after breakout close, or on same-day close
Exit Rules (First to trigger wins)
✓ Target hit: 10% profit — book 50%, trail rest
✓ Stop-loss hit: Exit 100%, no averaging down
✓ Time stop: 5 days without meaningful move — exit
✓ Breakdown below key EMA — exit regardless of P&L
Never hold through earnings without specific plan

Risk & Money Management

The only part of trading you fully control — master this before anything else

The Golden Rules (from Jack Schwager's Market Wizards)

1. Never risk more than 1-2% of capital on a single trade. On ₹5L capital = max ₹5,000–₹10,000 risk per trade.
2. Position size from your stop, not from a fixed amount. If stop is 5% away, your position = (2% capital risk) ÷ (5% stop) = 40% of capital max.
3. Maximum portfolio risk at any time: 6–8%. If in 4 trades each risking 2%, one bad day can't blow you up.
4. If you lose 10% of capital in a week, stop trading for 2 weeks. This prevents the emotional spiral that destroys accounts.

Trading Psychology

The market transfers money from the undisciplined to the disciplined. Period.

The 5 Killers of Retail Traders

1. Averaging Down Losers
Buying more of a stock that is going against you. The market doesn't know your average price. Stop out, wait for setup to reset.
2. Moving the Stop Loss
"Just a little more" is how small losses become account killers. Stop is placed by analysis, not emotion.
3. Overtrading After Loss
Revenge trading to recover quickly accelerates losses. One stop-out = stop for the day. Review, journal, come back tomorrow.
4. Selling Winners Too Early
Fear of giving back profit causes you to exit 3% when setup had 10% potential. Let the system work. Trail, don't panic-exit.
5. FOMO Entries
Chasing stocks already up 8-10% without a setup. You become the exit liquidity for those who bought right. Wait for the next pullback.

Building a Bulletproof Process

Pre-Market (8:00–9:00 AM)
Check SGX Nifty / GIFT Nifty. Note US market close. Check VIX. Review India VIX. Scan for overnight news on holdings.
Market Open (9:15–9:30 AM)
Watch, don't trade. Let opening volatility settle. See where Nifty is relative to yesterday's close.
Entry Window (9:30–11:30 AM)
Best time for breakout entries. Volume is high, institutions are active. Set limit orders, not market orders.
Afternoon Review (1:30–2:30 PM)
Mid-day lull. Review open positions. No new entries unless exceptional setup.
Close Watch (3:00–3:30 PM)
Watch for end-of-day strength or weakness. Closing price matters most. Set next day's alerts.
Post-Market (4:00–5:00 PM)
Journal: what worked, what didn't. Screenshot charts. Plan next day's watchlist. Emotion debrief.
